(Source: `area_partition_independent.txt` from `area_hier.rpt`. Rounding is to 4 decimals at the hierarchy level.)

- **Common storage dominates.** FIFO subtrees make up 92.40–98.63% of every case (Codex final numeric cross-check of the reported partitions). RR FIFO mean is ~19491 units per source; FCFS/Main is ~25428 units per source (TS_EN=1).
- **FCFS/Main overhead over RR is mostly arrival metadata.** About 95% of the FCFS−RR difference is in the FIFO subtrees: N·16·32 timestamp flip-flops plus their write/read muxing. The remainder is the selector, which is +869/+2255/+4770 units for N4/8/16, plus ~240–430 units of top-level arrival counter and adder logic.
- **Main versus FCFS** (same storage) costs +4.97–5.89% of the total. Almost all of it is the FAI policy subtree: B/S counters, 32-bit threshold arithmetic and the four-state classifier, on top of the shared selector inside it. The common hold controller differs by ≤ 51 units.
- **Scaling.** Totals scale ~4.0× from N4 to N16, as storage-bound linear scaling predicts. FCFS selector area grows 5.3×, which is superlinear (comparison tree).
- **Not pure selector cost.** These are differences of the complete mapped switch under one common method. Mapping and loading also shift common FIFO and hold subtrees by tens of units without any RTL change. Do not label total differences as pure selector or policy-logic cost.
- v2 areas must not be mixed with original v1 (`primary-20261020-v1-*`, other source and profile). The v1 dataset remains preserved and unchanged in Git.

## 4. Untested timing checks (separated from violations)

There are 140 untested checks in total, all `constant_disabled`, with 0 violated checks:

| RR N4/8/16, FCFS N16, Main N16 | 0 | — | — | — |

**Explanation**
- In some cases DC mapped flops onto synchronous set/reset cells, with the data steered through RSTB and the D pin tied to 0. The arrival counter is the main example.
- The timing check on the constant pin is then meaningless, while the live data pin stays timed. Setup-check arithmetic confirms this. In FCFS N4: 8824 DFFX1 + 32 DFFSSR × 3 data/control pins = 8920 setup checks. Of those, the 32 D checks are untested and the RSTB/SETB checks are tested and met.
- PT `report_disable_timing` shows only `p` (propagated-constant) arcs: 72/8/68/12/8/4/8/8/0 per case. No user, case-analysis or loop-breaking disables exist.
- **Classification:** benign, technology-mapping constant pins. These are not hidden violations or disabled checks.
- **Residual risk:** functional equivalence of this sequential remapping is **not formally proven**, because commercial equivalence is NOT_RUN (§8).

## 6. Findings

### Critical
None.

### Major
None that blocks comparative use.

### Minor / risks
- **MI-1, hold margin pre-CTS.** The smallest hold slacks are +0.0338 ns (FCFS N4) and +0.0399 ns (Main N4), on ideal clocks with 0.05 ns hold uncertainty. Clock-tree skew after placement could erode this. It is pre-layout evidence only.
- **MI-2, Main N16 setup margin** is +1.158 ns at 25 ns. It is the most physically sensitive case. A wire-load model is not extracted parasitics.
- **MI-3, cap margin is not universal.** All nine used-driver populations meet the original limits, with a minimum remaining margin of 12.6% (Main N8). This is measured per netlist. It does **not** prove that the 0.75 overlay is sufficient for other designs or that the 20 undefined-limit library outputs are safe in general. Those outputs are constant pseudo-drivers here and are excluded with a list.
- **MI-4, sequential remapping** onto DFFSSR cells (§4) is covered by STA but not by commercial equivalence checking.
- **MI-5, write_sdc rounding** of the output load (2.656828 → 2.65683 fF) is uniform across v1, the pilots and v2. Informational.
- **MI-6, DC lint noise** (VER-941 in non-elaborated `$fatal` guards) is a cosmetic RTL cleanup candidate. Any RTL edit would require full regression and a new sweep, so none is recommended now.
